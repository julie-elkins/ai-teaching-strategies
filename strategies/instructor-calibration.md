# Instructor calibration

## Strategy

Align facilitators on the same observable teaching behaviors before scaling a
course, lab, or train-the-trainer program.

## Audience and context

Use this when more than one person will deliver a technical lesson or review
learner work.

## Teaching problem

Without calibration, two facilitators can give different guidance, tolerate
different safety boundaries, or interpret the same learner artifact differently.

## Facilitator move

Use a fixed observer rubric covering technical accuracy, hands-on execution,
audience adaptation, live debugging, accessibility, and safe escalation. Review
synthetic examples together, surface disagreements, and record the coaching
action rather than hiding uncertainty in a readiness score.

## Learner evidence

Track the lesson revision, facilitator decision, observed behavior, and agreed
next practice. Keep the record separate from learner-sensitive content.

## Feedback and adaptation

Recalibrate when the rubric changes, the technical environment changes, or
facilitators disagree on a high-risk behavior. Do not treat one observation as a
permanent capability judgment.

## Accessibility

Allow written, live, and recorded delivery formats to demonstrate the same
behavior. Make the rubric explicit before observation.

## Responsible-use boundary

Calibration is quality support, not an automated employment, grading, or
promotion decision. Consequential decisions require a human owner and a
declared evidence standard.

## Verification

Have facilitators independently rate the same synthetic delivery or learner
artifact, compare disagreements, and document the resolved interpretation.

## What this does and does not prove

It proves that facilitators discussed and aligned on the declared rubric. It
does not prove teaching quality across all audiences or conditions.
